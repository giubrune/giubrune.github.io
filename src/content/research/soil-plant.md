Plants draw water from the soil, and with it any chemicals dissolved in that water. Some of these, such as pharmaceuticals that reach agricultural soils, can be taken up by roots and moved to leaves and fruits. Predicting how much reaches the parts of the plant that we eat requires following water and solutes across the whole system, from the soil pores to the fruit.

## In more detail

This research develops mechanistic models that couple variably saturated flow and transport in the soil with the plant’s own water and solute dynamics. The Dynamic Plant Uptake module for HYDRUS describes the translocation and transformation of chemicals in the soil–plant continuum. It has been tested against detailed experiments, for example on the uptake of carbamazepine by green pea plants, and extended into a multiscale biophysical model for ionizable compounds.

More recent work models the allocation of water and carbohydrates along the soil–fruit axis, as a step towards a unified, physically based soil–plant model (“re-greening HYDRUS”).
